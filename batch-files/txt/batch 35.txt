PROBLEM 6A-1 REINFORCED AND UNREINFORCED MASONRY WALL DESIGN
Problem 6A-1a: Design a reinforced masonry wall for an exterior blast load.
Procedure:
Step 1. Establish design parameters.
a. Pressure-time loading
b. Structural configuration including geometry, support conditions, and 
required Protection Category of wall.
Step 2. Select masonry unit size and the size and spacing of the primary steel 
reinforcement (i.e. vertical steel reinforcing bars) and secondary joint 
reinforcement. Also determine the static design strengths for the masonry 
and the steel reinforcement (Section 6-7.3). 
Step 3. Calculate the dynamic design strengths of the masonry and steel 
reinforcement, using the static strengths from Step 2 and Table 6-2 (+).
Step 4. Using the dynamic design strengths from Step 3, the size and spacing of 
reinforcement, and d, the depth of the reinforcement, determine the 
ultimate moment capacity of the wall (Equation 6-2). Check that maximum 
and minimum requirements for reinforcement ratio and reinforcement 
spacing are met.
Step 5. Determine the ultimate resistance of the wall using the ultimate moment 
capacity of Step 4 and the equations of Table 3-1 (+) for one-way 
spanning elements.
Step 6. Determine the average moment of inertia based on In and Ic (Equation 6-
10). Use Equation 6-1 to find the net moment of inertia of the masonry 
section In. Use Equation 6-11 and the depth of the reinforcement to 
calculate the moment of inertia of the cracked section Ic.
Step 7. Calculate the modulus of elasticity of the masonry unit Em from Equation 
6-2 and the masonry unit compression strength of Step 2.
Step 8. Use the average moment of inertia from Step 6, the modulus of elasticity 
from Step 7, and the equations from Table 3-8 (+) to find the equivalent 
elastic stiffness. 
Step 9. Determine the equivalent elastic yield deflection using the ultimate 
resistance (Step 5), the equivalent stiffness (Step 8), and Equation 3-36.

PROBLEM 6A-1B: ANALYZE AN UNREINFORCED MASONRY WALL FOR AN 
EXTERIOR BLAST LOAD.
Procedure:
Step 1. Establish design parameters.
a. Pressure-time loading. 
b. Structural configuration including geometry, support conditions, and 
required Protection Category of wall.
Step 2. Determine masonry unit size. Also determine a conservative estimate for 
the static strength for the masonry (Section 6-7.3). 
Step 3. Calculate the dynamic design compressive and flexural strengths of the 
masonry using the static strength from Step 2, Table 6-2 (+), and Section 
6-7.3.3. 
Step 4. Using the dynamic flexural strength from Step 3, determine the ultimate 
moment capacity of the wall (Equation 6-12). Use cross sectional 
properties from Table 6-1 (+).
Step 5. Calculate the ultimate flexural resistance of the wall using the ultimate 
moment capacity of Step 4 and the equations of Table 3-1 (+) for one-way 
spanning elements. 
Step 6. Determine the moment of inertia of the masonry section from Table 6-1
(+).
Step 7. Calculate the modulus of elasticity of the masonry unit Em from Equation 
6-2 and the masonry unit compression strength of Step 2.
Step 8. Use the moment of inertia from Step 6, the modulus of elasticity from Step 
7, and the equations from Table 3-8 (+) to find the elastic stiffness. 
Step 9. Determine the elastic yield deflection using the ultimate resistance (Step 
5), the stiffness (Step 8), and Equation 3-36.
Step 10. Calculate the areal mass (mass divided by the blast loaded area) of the 
wall. 
Step 11. Calculate the ultimate resistance from axial load arching using Equation 6-
13.
Step 12. Construct the resistance-deflection curve for the wall response based on 
Figure 6-13 (+).

Step 13. Determine the load-mass factor, KLM, for each stiffness region of the 
resistance-deflection curve. 
Step 14. Calculate the natural period of vibration from Equation 3-60, using the

elastic flexural response from Step 13.
Step 15. Use a time-stepping dynamic analysis to determine the wall response. 
This is necessary because of the complex shape of the resistance￾deflection curve. Use a time-stepping method to solve the equation of

the wall at each time step and use numerical integration to determine the

more explanation. Apply this method until a maximum deflection Xm is

is larger than that permitted, the wall must be structurally upgraded.

based on the maximum resistance of the wall from Step 15. Calculate the 
out-of-plane shear capacity based on the shear area (Equation 6-4) and 
shear strength (Equation 6-3). If the shear force exceeds the shear 
capacity, the wall must be upgraded.
Step 18. Use the ultimate flexural resistance from Step 5 and equations in Table 3-
9 (+) to calculate the ultimate shear load at the support for inbound 
response. Use this shear load as the connection load for inbound and
rebound response. Compare this load to the capacity of the wall 
connections to its supports. Design upgrades for the wall connections if 
necessary.

wall. Check that the combined in-plane and out-of-plane shear satisfy the 
interaction formula (Equation 6-7).
